# Knowledge

The Knowledge Layer organizes, connects, and governs engineering knowledge throughout the DunderCode Engineering System (DESys).

While the Foundation establishes the principles that guide engineering, the Knowledge Layer transforms those principles into a structured and interconnected body of reusable engineering assets.

Rather than treating engineering documents as isolated artifacts, DESys organizes them as part of a coherent knowledge ecosystem where every asset contributes to the evolution of the platform.

The Knowledge Layer provides the semantic foundation that enables engineering knowledge to remain discoverable, traceable, reusable, and continuously evolving.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of the Knowledge Layer is to transform engineering experience into structured knowledge.

Engineering decisions, standards, reviews, architectural records, guides, and specifications are organized into a unified knowledge system that supports both human understanding and AI-assisted engineering.

By establishing explicit relationships between engineering assets, DESys ensures that knowledge remains connected rather than fragmented.

---

# Knowledge Domains

The Knowledge Layer is composed of specialized domains, each responsible for a distinct category of engineering knowledge.

| Domain | Responsibility |
|--------|----------------|
| **Architecture** | Defines the structural organization of engineering knowledge and platform architecture. |
| **DEKG** | Establishes the semantic knowledge graph connecting engineering assets. |
| **DAR** | Records documentation and engineering review outcomes. |
| **ADR** | Captures software architecture decisions. |
| **RFC** | Documents engineering proposals and planned changes. |
| **Guides** | Provides practical engineering guidance and operational procedures. |

Additional domains may be introduced as the engineering platform evolves.

# Semantic Relationships

A defining characteristic of the Knowledge Layer is the explicit relationship between engineering assets.

Rather than existing independently, engineering documents reference, complement, and validate one another.

Examples include:

- A standard referencing its governing methodology.
- An architectural decision linked to a related proposal.
- A review validating a specification.
- A guide illustrating the application of a standard.

These relationships form the semantic network that underpins the DunderCode Engineering Knowledge Graph (DEKG).

---

# Knowledge Evolution

Engineering knowledge is expected to evolve continuously.

Every validated engineering experience contributes to improving the platform.

The Knowledge Layer supports this evolution through:

- Structured documentation.
- Version-controlled engineering assets.
- Semantic traceability.
- Peer review.
- Continuous validation.
- Knowledge reuse.

This process transforms isolated engineering experience into long-term organizational knowledge.
