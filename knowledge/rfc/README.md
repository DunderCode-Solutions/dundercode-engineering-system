# Request for Comments (RFC)

The Request for Comments (RFC) Domain defines the proposal model used to evolve the DunderCode Engineering System (DESys).

An RFC captures a proposed engineering change before implementation, providing a structured process for presenting ideas, discussing alternatives, gathering feedback, and reaching informed decisions.

Rather than documenting completed work, RFCs document engineering proposals and serve as the starting point for controlled platform evolution.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of the RFC Domain is to establish a transparent and collaborative process for proposing engineering changes.

Significant modifications to standards, architecture, methodologies, tooling, or engineering practices should be introduced through structured proposals rather than informal discussions.

By documenting proposals before implementation, DESys promotes thoughtful decision-making, broad participation, and long-term architectural consistency.

---

# What is an RFC?

A Request for Comments (RFC) is an engineering asset that describes a proposed change to the platform.

An RFC typically includes:

- The problem or opportunity.
- The proposed solution.
- Expected benefits.
- Alternatives considered.
- Potential risks and trade-offs.
- Open questions for discussion.

An RFC represents a proposal—not a final decision.

Once accepted, its outcomes may lead to one or more Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) and subsequent implementation.

# Proposal Lifecycle

Engineering proposals follow a structured lifecycle.

```text
Engineering Opportunity
        │
        ▼
Request for Comments
        │
        ▼
Discussion & Feedback
        │
        ▼
Decision
        │
        ▼
Architecture Decision Record
        │
        ▼
Implementation
```

This lifecycle ensures that significant engineering changes are evaluated before they become part of the platform.

---

# Proposal Principles

RFCs within DESys follow a common set of principles.

## Problem-Oriented

Every proposal should clearly explain the problem it intends to solve.

## Open Discussion

RFCs encourage collaborative review and constructive feedback.

## Evidence-Based

Proposals should be supported by technical reasoning and, when appropriate, empirical evidence.

## Transparency

The decision-making process should remain visible throughout the proposal lifecycle.

## Traceability

Accepted or rejected proposals should remain part of the engineering history.

## Evolutionary Design

RFCs support the continuous evolution of DESys without disrupting architectural consistency.

# Final Thought

Engineering excellence depends not only on making good decisions, but also on creating a disciplined process for proposing change.
