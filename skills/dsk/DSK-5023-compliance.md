# 1. Purpose

This skill defines the **Engineering Compliance Governance (ECG)** discipline adopted by the DunderCode Engineering System (DESys).

Within DESys, compliance is not merely adherence to regulations or periodic audit preparation.

It is the engineering discipline responsible for continuously governing engineering obligations through verifiable controls, evidence, assessments and traceable engineering decisions.

Compliance demonstrates engineering trustworthiness.

---

# 2. Scope

Engineering Compliance Governance governs:

* Engineering Obligations
* Compliance Controls
* Compliance Assessments
* Compliance Evidence
* Compliance Mapping
* Compliance Automation
* Compliance Traceability

# 5. Engineering Compliance Model (ECM)

DESys adopts the **Engineering Compliance Model (ECM)**.

Every engineering obligation SHALL define:

* Obligation
* Source
* Scope
* Engineering Control
* Responsible Owner
* Evidence
* Verification
* Compliance Status
* Exceptions
* Traceability

The ECM defines the canonical compliance model adopted by DESys.

---

DESys adopts the **Compliance Mapping Model (CMM)**.

Example:


Compliance Mapping minimizes duplication while strengthening engineering consistency.

DESys promotes Compliance-as-Code whenever technically feasible.

Engineering compliance rules SHOULD be:

# 14. AI Compliance Analysis

AI MAY automatically evaluate:

* obligations without controls;
* controls without evidence;
* expired evidence;
* pending exceptions;
* incomplete implementations;
* overlapping compliance requirements;
* compliance impact caused by engineering changes.

Recommendations SHALL remain deterministic and evidence-based.

---

# 15. Engineering Rules

Engineering Compliance Governance MUST:

* assign ownership to every obligation;
* associate every obligation with engineering controls;
* preserve supporting evidence;
* maintain complete traceability;
* support continuous verification.

Engineering Compliance Governance MUST NOT:

* rely exclusively on manual audits;
* maintain undocumented obligations;
* approve compliance without evidence;
* disconnect obligations from implemented controls;
* lose engineering history.
